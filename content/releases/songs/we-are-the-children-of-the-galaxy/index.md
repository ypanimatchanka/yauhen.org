Some songs appear not out of inspiration, but out of obligation — to something that once taught you how to dream. *“We Are the Children of the Galaxy”* *(russian: “Мы дети галактики”)* is one of them.

It grew from melodies that have somehow settled in the air of an entire generation:

- *“The Grass by the House”* (russian: “Трава у дома” by *Zemlyane*) — a hymn to homesickness and the pull of Earth;
- *“There Is Only a Moment”* (russian: “Есть только миг” from *The Land of Sannikov* — “Земля Санникова”);
- *“The Beautiful Faraway”* (russian: “Прекрасное далеко” from *Guest from the Future* — “Гостья из будущего”).

They were all different in sound and style, yet shared a single idea: **a person can travel anywhere, as long as they remember why they want to return home.**

It’s written from the point of view of someone looking through a spacecraft window, and realising they’re not missing the Earth itself, but the version of themselves who once lived there.

It’s not a song about space; it’s a song about memory. About that fleeting moment between past and future, where we still manage to feel at home.

Every line here knows where it came from — from those films and songs where people dreamt not of speed or glory, but of not going dim along the way.

This is a song that might have been written in 1983, but was only recorded now. It remembers the past, and sounds like a memory of the future.
